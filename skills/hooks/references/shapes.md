A hook is a pattern with the blanks filled in: keep the shape, swap in your own detail. These are starting points. You can blend two in one line, and you'll find new ones as you go (see "Your shapes" at the end).

Each shape below has a one-liner, when it fits, what to watch out for, and real examples: one from AI and one from outside it, so you can see the shape works in any niche. The hook is quoted word for word ("..." marks a cut).

Where the shapes come from: six from Kallaway ([The 6 Hook Archetypes That Consistently Outperform](https://www.content.game/p/hooks)), the curiosity gap and the versus from Paddy Galloway ([The New Rules of YouTube, Colin and Samir](https://www.colinandsamir.com/resources/the-new-rules-of-youtube-from-paddy-galloway)), and the list from Alex Hormozi (*$100M Leads*).

## 1. The contrarian

**One-liner:** say the opposite of what your audience has been told, so they stay to argue or to be proven wrong.
**Fits when:** your niche has well-known advice or trends that are out of date, and you've done the opposite and can show it.
**Watch out:** only use it when it's true for you. A take you can't back up reads as bait.

- Nate Herk (@nateherkai), AI, [Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DdZIw9PkyyQ/): "Stop prompting Claude. Andrej Karpathy thinks there's a much better way to work with AI..."
- The Fitness Chef (@thefitnesschef_), nutrition, [TikTok](https://www.tiktok.com/@thefitnesschef_/video/7475071826465492246): "5 diet trends that need to die" (text on screen, no voice)

## 2. The investigator

**One-liner:** reveal a hidden thing most people miss: you read the fine print, the label or the data.
**Fits when:** there's breaking news in your niche, or you found something in the details. Blend it with the latest news on your topic.
**Watch out:** it falls flat if the secret is something everyone already knows.

- Nick Saraev (@nick_saraev), AI, [Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DdKA61hvQKJ/): "Someone just leaked the full system prompt from Claude Fable 5.1..."
- Bobby Parrish (@bobbyparrish), groceries, [TikTok](https://www.tiktok.com/@bobbyparrish/video/7672457113779604766): "Here's why I would never buy ground beef at Aldi, and it's not for the reason you think."

## 3. The teacher

**One-liner:** promise a fix for a problem they already know they have: here's how to do this.
**Fits when:** people already want the result, for example a trend everyone is curious how to build.
**Watch out:** deliver on the promise. If you say they'll learn it in 3 minutes, they must have learned it by the end, or it hurts your name as an educator. It's the safest shape and the easiest to overuse.

- Kevin Fremon (@kevinfremon), AI, [TikTok](https://www.tiktok.com/@kevinfremon/video/7655048241062464782): "I'm going to show you how you can build your very own AI Jarvis in under 3 minutes."
- Jeremy Ethier (@jeremyethier), fitness, [TikTok](https://www.tiktok.com/@jeremyethier/video/7198936808133020933): "Deadlifts are a great exercise to build the entire backside of the body, but only if you do them right."

## 4. The experimenter

**One-liner:** try something and report back what you found, so they stay for the result.
**Fits when:** you're testing or tinkering anyway, so you capture the process and get a video out of work you were doing already. Takes longer to make.
**Watch out:** honest results beat impressive ones. Only say "I tried it" once you have.

- Tunde Alao (@itstundeanthony), AI, [Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DdWmIHTC322/): "Fact or Hype?" on screen over a clip of the claim, then he tests it himself and gives a verdict
- Guga Foods (@gugafoods), cooking, [TikTok](https://www.tiktok.com/@gugafoods/video/7655191050251767070): "I aged a steak for 4 years and now I'm gonna eat it."

## 5. The fortune teller

**One-liner:** point at a future they can picture and you believe will come true, then help them get ready.
**Fits when:** you have a real prediction about your audience's work, money or industry. Everyone cares about their own future.
**Watch out:** skip it when you're only guessing. It blends well with the contrarian (a future that isn't what most people expect).

- Brand Nat (@brandnat), AI, [TikTok](https://www.tiktok.com/@brandnat/video/7686477042241572117): "SaaS is now dead is probably what most people are thinking, but I would challenge you to think about services."
- Humphrey Yang (@humphreytalks), money, [TikTok](https://www.tiktok.com/@humphreytalks/video/7650563695734443294): "If you have $100,000 invested by age 25, you could stop saving for retirement forever and you'd still retire a millionaire."

## 6. The magician

**One-liner:** stop the scroll with something to look at: a before and after, a screen, a result already showing.
**Fits when:** the thing is worth seeing, like software running on its own or an object that looks wrong.
**Watch out:** on a short, the first frame has to carry the hook on its own. Line up the shot before you write the words.

- Riley Brown (@rileybrown.ai), AI, [TikTok](https://www.tiktok.com/@rileybrown.ai/video/7608016811770137886): "Oh my God. OpenClaw, which is running on this Mac mini, can now control Blender."
- Zach King (@zachking), magic, [TikTok](https://www.tiktok.com/@zachking/video/7661646300332494094): "Is it cake or is it fake?" (first frame: a knife over two phones)

## 7. The curiosity gap

**One-liner:** open a gap in what they know and leave it open, so they stay to see it filled.
**Fits when:** almost any teaching video: you have something to say, so tease that it will help them. The safe fallback when no other shape stands out. Reusable frame: "This is the craziest [your niche] fact I know."
**Watch out:** close the gap fast, or it reads as clickbait.

- Kevin Stratvert (@kevinstratvert), AI at work, [TikTok](https://www.tiktok.com/@kevinstratvert/video/7636057620234767629): "Shh, don't tell your boss about this Excel trick. Watch this."
- Mark Rober (@markrober), science, [TikTok](https://www.tiktok.com/@markrober/video/7349188326760516907): "This is the craziest fact I know."

## 8. The versus

**One-liner:** put two things side by side and make them pick.
**Fits when:** people really choose between the options: tools, products, prices, methods.
**Watch out:** both sides have to be real choices people make, never a strawman.

- Jeff Su (@jsushie), AI, [TikTok](https://www.tiktok.com/@jsushie/video/7611493927685721345): "I use ChatGPT, Gemini, and Claude almost every day, and each one has a specific superpower."
- Max the Meat Guy (@maxthemeatguy), food, [TikTok](https://www.tiktok.com/@maxthemeatguy/video/7402668711666142494): "What does a 5-year-old like more? $1 pasta or $5,000 pasta?"

## 9. The list

**One-liner:** promise a payoff they can count, so they know the length and the value up front.
**Fits when:** the video has a few separate tips, tools or steps. On a short, viewers know a small list will take about a minute, so it feels like good value for the time.
**Watch out:** small numbers beat big ones. On a short, keep it to about 9 or fewer. Promise 50 and people assume the video is long and scroll.

- Greg Isenberg (@gregisenberg), AI, [TikTok](https://www.tiktok.com/@gregisenberg/video/7660144437326023966): "These 3 Claude Code plugins can stop Claude from wasting tokens."
- Ali Abdaal (@aliabdaal), business, [TikTok](https://www.tiktok.com/@aliabdaal/video/7616706222288424225): "If you wanna make passive income, these are 3 things I wish I knew before I got started."

## Bonus: the visual hook

**One-liner:** the first frame does the work before you say a word, so people stop for the picture alone. It sits on top of any shape above.
**Fits when:** always worth a thought. Tie it to your brand, so a repeat look (a prop, a colour, a framing) tells people it's you before they read your name. Works well for faceless videos too.
**Watch out:** it doesn't have to be forced, and you can switch it up as you go.

Three examples by Jay E (@robonuggets), each with the same look: an orange on a chopstick as a pointer, over a screen already showing the result.

- [TikTok](https://www.tiktok.com/@robonuggets/video/7506869284480503047): first frame an AI news anchor already playing, then "It's actually over and Google just won everything."
- [TikTok](https://www.tiktok.com/@robonuggets/video/7409460970231172360): first frame a video call with an AI face in it, then "Zoom calls will never be the same again."
- [TikTok](https://www.tiktok.com/@robonuggets/video/7462595444984614152): first frame the ChatGPT logo, then Blender, then "Did ChatGPT and Blender just have a baby?"

## Find new hooks

Scroll your feed on the account you post from, so it's tuned to your niche. Whenever a hook stops your scroll, bookmark it, then send it to your agent with "add this hook" and the link. Accounts that break viral videos down help too, for example [The Post Protocol](https://www.instagram.com/thepostprotocol/) on Instagram, which shows a hook that worked and how to copy its pattern.
